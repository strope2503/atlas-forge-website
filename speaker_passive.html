Wood Assembly & Raptor Liner Bedliner Installation

Proper box sealing and armor application guarantee weatherproofing against rain, dust, and humidity.

Step 1: Dry Fit & Pre-Drilling

Clamp your cut 1/2" MDF panels together without glue to verify alignment. Pre-drill screw holes to prevent the MDF edges from splitting.

Step 2: Airtight Gluing & Sealing

Assemble the box using wood glue and exterior screws. Crucial Step: Run a continuous bead of thick silicone caulk along every internal corner seam. A passive radiator system relies on an airtight seal to function correctly; any air leaks will ruin bass response.

Step 3: Applying the Raptor Liner Finish
  1. Surface Prep: Lightly sand the exterior MDF surfaces with 80-grit sandpaper to give the bedliner grip, then wipe clean of all dust.
  2. Mixing: Pour the Raptor bedliner base and hardener components together according to kit instructions, shaking vigorously for 2 minutes.
  3. Application: Using the included textured roller (or a schutz spray gun), apply an even, heavy first coat across all exterior walls. Pay special attention to corners and edges.
  4. Curing: Let the first coat tack up for 60 minutes, apply a second coat for full texture depth, and allow the box to cure fully for 24 hours in the garage before installing electronics or hardware.

Dummy-Proof Electrical Wiring Sequence

Follow this exact chain to ensure clean power delivery, low-voltage battery protection, and crisp stereo separation.

Safety First: Never have a DeWalt battery plugged into the dock while stripping wires or soldering connections.
Step 1: Battery Dock to Voltage Cutoff

Run the Positive (Red 14 AWG) wire from the DeWalt dock fuse holder directly to the IN+ terminal on the XH-M609 module. Run the Negative (Black 14 AWG) wire directly to the IN- terminal.

Programming Note: Set the XH-M609 cutoff threshold to 15.5V to protect your DeWalt lithium cells from deep discharge.

Step 2: Cutoff Module to Master Switch

Run a Positive wire from the OUT+ of the XH-M609 to one terminal pole of your heavy-duty DPDT toggle switch. Run a Negative wire from OUT- straight to the amplifier ground.

Step 3: Switch to Amplifier Power Terminals

Connect the switched output from the toggle switch to the DC Power Input (+) screw terminal on the Dayton KABT-250 amplifier board.

Step 4: Speaker Channel Output

Run 16 AWG speaker wire from the Left (+/-) terminals on the KABT-250 to the left Pioneer 6x9 driver, and from the Right (+/-) terminals to the right Pioneer driver. Secure all wire joints with heat-shrink tubing and hot glue.

Setting Up On-The-Fly Preset Switching

You can configure multiple profiles in SigmaStudio and assign them to an external momentary push button wired to the GPIO control pins on the KABT-250 board:

  • Preset 1 (Garage Mode - Enclosed): Flat response with a slight -2 dB cut at 80 Hz to prevent acoustic muddying off concrete garage walls.
  • Preset 2 (Backyard / Field Mode - Open Air): Full +3 dB low-shelf boost at 60 Hz to compensate for open-air bass roll-off.

Persistence: Right-click the ADAU1701 chip block in Hardware Configuration and select Write Latest Compilation to E2PROM to save both profiles permanently offline.

Optional ESP32 Smart Battery Telemetry

If you want to upgrade your boombox with remote wireless battery monitoring and voltage alerts via Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), you can wire a low-cost ESP32 microcontroller into your power rail.

Hardware Hookup

Tap into the DC power line right after the XH-M609 cutoff module. Use a small 5V step-down buck converter (LM2596) to power the ESP32 logic board. Connect the battery positive rail through a voltage divider resistor circuit (e.g., 100kΩ and 30kΩ) to GPIO 34 (ADC1_CH6) on the ESP32 to safely read up to 21V.

Sample Arduino / ESP32 Firmware Sketch

#include <BleKeyboard.h> // Or standard BLE server libraries const int analogInPin = 34; // Analog input pin connected to voltage divider float sensorValue = 0; float voltage = 0; float R1 = 100000.0; // 100k resistor float R2 = 30000.0; // 30k resistor void setup() { Serial.begin(115200); Serial.println("ATLAS FORGE Telemetry Initialized"); } void loop() { int raw = analogRead(analogInPin); // Convert ADC reading (0-4095) to voltage based on 3.3V reference and divider ratio sensorValue = (raw * 3.3) / 4095.0; voltage = sensorValue / (R2 / (R1 + R2)); Serial.print("DeWalt Pack Voltage: "); Serial.print(voltage); Serial.println(" V"); if (voltage < 15.2) { Serial.println("WARNING: Low Voltage Threshold Reached!"); } delay(5000); // Check every 5 seconds }

Outdoor Maintenance & Care Guide

Keep your ATLAS FORGE Pro-Acoustic boombox performing like new across garage work sessions, backyard cookouts, and soccer field trips.

1. Raptor Armor Care

The textured bedliner is nearly indestructible, but mud, grass clippings, or dirt can settle into the grain texture. Wipe down the exterior with a damp microfiber cloth and mild automotive soap as needed. Avoid harsh petroleum solvents.

2. DeWalt Battery Contact Maintenance

Check the spring-loaded copper contacts inside your 3D-printed dock periodically. If dust or moisture buildup appears, clean them with a cotton swab dipped in isopropyl alcohol (IPA) to maintain clean electrical conductivity and prevent voltage drop under heavy bass.

3. Seasonal Driver Inspection

Inspect the rubber surrounds on both the Pioneer 6x9s and the rear Dayton passive radiators once a season to ensure the sealing gaskets remain compressed tightly against the MDF cabinet.
